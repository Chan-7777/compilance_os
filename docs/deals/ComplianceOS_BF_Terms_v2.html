Before We Begin

Benedict, thank you for the detailed counter-proposal. The fact that you invested time restructuring the terms tells me you're serious about this — and so am I.

I agree with your framing: "We're both solving the same problem — friction in export transactions." And I agree this can become a category-defining model. That's exactly why these terms need to be right — not just for today, but for a partnership that compounds over years.

I've gone through each of your 8 clauses. Here's my honest response:

My guiding principle: Every clause should pass a simple test — does it create a partnership where both sides are more valuable together than apart? If one side can walk away with the other's value, it's not a partnership — it's a vendor contract.

Clause-by-Clause Response

Accepted Clause 1 — Positioning
"Ben Finserv = primary client acquisition, advisory, and transaction orchestration. ComplianceOS = compliance intelligence and documentation infrastructure provider."

Our position: Agreed. This is consistent with our original framework. You own the client relationship and advisory practice. We provide the intelligence infrastructure. Clean separation.

Counter Clause 2 — Client Ownership & Non-Solicitation
"All exporter relationships sourced through Ben Finserv remain exclusive to Ben Finserv. ComplianceOS shall not solicit these clients for 24 months post engagement."

What we accept:

  • Client advisory relationships sourced by you stay with you. Full stop.
  • We will not offer trade finance advisory or structuring to your clients — that's your domain.

What we counter:

24-month non-solicitation scope needs clarification.

When an exporter creates a ComplianceOS account (even via your funnel), they become a ComplianceOS platform user. We need the right to:
  • Send product updates and feature announcements
  • Provide ongoing compliance support for their existing shipments
  • Offer compliance services for new markets/products they expand into

Proposed language: "ComplianceOS will not offer trade finance structuring, lending, or financial advisory services to Ben Finserv-sourced clients during the partnership and for 12 months (not 24) post-termination. ComplianceOS retains the right to provide compliance services and platform access to all registered users."

Rationale: Your clients use our platform daily for compliance. Blocking us from communicating with our own users for 24 months creates operational problems and hurts the exporter experience — which ultimately hurts your deals too.

Counter Clause 3 — Data Ownership
"All transaction-level data, buyer risk insights, deal performance data, and funding outcomes — jointly accessible, with unrestricted usage rights for Ben Finserv." [NON-NEGOTIABLE]

What we accept:

  • Full joint access to all data generated through the partnership
  • You get complete visibility into compliance scoring, risk data, and deal outcomes
  • You can use this data for your advisory practice, client reporting, and deal structuring

What we counter:

"Unrestricted usage rights" is too broad.

That language would allow either party to feed partnership data into a competing service, license it to third parties, or use it to train competing AI models.

Proposed language: "Both parties shall have full access to all partnership-generated data for the purpose of:
  • Servicing joint clients
  • Internal analytics and reporting
  • Improving their respective platforms
Neither party shall license, sell, or provide partnership data to direct competitors."

This gives you everything you need operationally — full access, full analytics, full usage for your business — while preventing either of us from accidentally arming a competitor.

Counter Clause 4 — Revenue Model
Tiered flat fees: < ₹2 Cr → ₹10,000 | ₹2–5 Cr → ₹20,000 | ₹5 Cr+ → ₹30,000. "Fee payable only upon successful closure and disbursement." "Fee applicable only if ComplianceOS inputs materially contributed to deal closure."

What we accept:

  • Moving from percentage to tiered flat fees — simpler, more predictable for both sides
  • Fee payable only upon successful deal closure — we're aligned on performance-based earning

What we counter:

Regarding "material contribution" clause — we need to remove this.

If a ComplianceOS Deal Pack was generated and presented to a lender, that's material contribution. Creating a subjective "did it help?" evaluation opens the door to disputes on every deal. The trigger should be simple: Was a ComplianceOS Deal Pack used in the deal? → Fee applies.

Context: On a ₹5Cr deal, your finance fee is ₹2.5–5L. Our ₹50K counter is 10–20% of your fee — still very reasonable for the compliance intelligence and bank-ready documentation that accelerated that closure.

Discuss Clause 5 — Platform Dependency / Lock-in
"ComplianceOS shall be the preferred compliance partner, but Ben Finserv retains the right to use alternative compliance workflows where required."

The concern: This clause, combined with Clause 8 (36-month non-compete on ComplianceOS), creates an asymmetric lock:

Proposed middle ground:

"ComplianceOS shall be the exclusive compliance platform for all exporters onboarded through the joint funnel. Ben Finserv may use alternative compliance workflows for clients sourced entirely outside this partnership."

This is fair because: If we're investing in API integration, co-branding, and pipeline support for your funnel, those clients should flow through ComplianceOS. You remain free to use other tools for clients outside our partnership.
Accepted Clause 6 — Deal Pack Co-Branding
"Co-branded, with Ben Finserv as structuring authority and ComplianceOS as compliance intelligence provider."

Our position: Agreed. You structure the deal, we provide the compliance intelligence. Both brands visible. Both clearly credited for their domain.

Accepted Clause 7 — Lender Relationship Clause
"All lender relationships, deal structuring methodologies, and financial advisory processes remain exclusive domain of Ben Finserv."

Our position: Agreed in full. Lending relationships and deal structuring methodology are your IP and your domain. We have no interest in entering this space.

Discuss Clause 8 — Non-Compete (36 Months)
"ComplianceOS shall not directly or indirectly enter trade finance structuring, lender aggregation, or advisory services for exporters introduced via Ben Finserv for 36 months."

What we accept:

  • ComplianceOS will not enter trade finance structuring or lender aggregation — this is your expertise, not ours
  • A reasonable non-compete duration is appropriate

What we counter:

36 months is disproportionate. Our counter: 12 months, mutual.

  • Our original proposal was 12 months — that's already substantial in a fast-moving market
  • "Mutual" means we add: Ben Finserv shall not build, acquire, or commission a competing compliance intelligence platform for the same 12-month period
  • The non-compete should apply equally — if ComplianceOS can't enter your space, you shouldn't enter ours
Flexibility note: We're open to extending the mutual non-compete to 24 months if the partnership includes either (a) a guaranteed minimum of 5 deals per quarter flowing through the joint funnel, or (b) acceptance of the higher-tier deal enablement fees proposed in Clause 4. Longer commitment from both sides should come with stronger commitment from both sides.

For context: Most Indian partnership agreements use 12–18 month non-competes. 36 months is closer to what you'd see in an acquisition, not a partnership. We're happy to commit to staying out of trade finance — but we need the same assurance on compliance tech.

The Bigger Picture

You said something in your counter-proposal that I completely agree with:

"Together, we can control the entire trade execution stack — not just participate in it."

That's exactly right. But controlling a stack requires both layers to be strong, invested, and protected.

If the compliance layer is treated as a replaceable vendor, the stack collapses.

Here's what I want you to consider:

ComplianceOS is not a commodity. There is no other live, deployed compliance intelligence platform in India that does what we do — not at any price. We built risk scoring across 190 countries, 33-point compliance checklists, FTA calculators with real tariff data, CBAM readiness modules, document generators, and a pre-shipment gate check. This took 12+ months and thousands of hours. It's not replaceable by a Google Sheet and a CA.

When your compliance layer is strong, your finance deals close faster, with better documentation, and with higher bank confidence. That's not infrastructure support — that's deal velocity.

The terms should reflect that both layers are essential. I'm not asking for more than my share. I'm asking for terms that make ComplianceOS as committed to your success as you are — because when we both benefit from every deal, we both push harder.
