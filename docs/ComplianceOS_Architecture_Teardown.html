What I would do, in order

Ranked by damage prevented per hour spent. The first two are correctness, not performance.

1

Delete two of the three MFN tables and make the deal pack read Postgres

The bank-facing PDF and the lender API are the two places a wrong duty figure costs you a relationship, and they are the two that never read the real data. One shared lookup, one source, three consumers.

Fig 4 · correctness · hours not days
2

Move the gate decision into an edge function and write the row

The compliance_gates table is already there and empty. Persisting the verdict is what lets the lender API stop carrying its own copy of the risk model, and it makes the gate result something a CA can audit after the fact.

Fig 3 · unblocks move 1 · a few days
3

Give shipments a real document identity

The invoice number is the natural key tying the shipping bill, the realisation certificate, the IGST claim and the RoDTEP entitlement together. Today it is a nullable link and four manual re-entries. This is the same finding as the invoice question the exporter asked.

Fig 2 · data model · the biggest UX win
4

Split the bundle by route

Every exporter downloads all nineteen screens to see the dashboard. Lazy loading the views is a contained change with no behavioural risk, and it matters on the mobile connections your users are actually on.

Fig 1 · 946 KB · an afternoon
5

Collapse to one migration system and delete the orphans

Three migrations exist in both directories, which means nobody can tell what a fresh database gets. Delete the loose files, keep the timestamped folder, and remove the two edge functions nothing calls.

Housekeeping · removes ambiguity, not code
6

Leave api.ts alone until the moves above shrink it

Splitting a 1,511-line file is the tempting first move and the wrong one. Once the gate check, the rate lookup and the PDF builders move server-side, most of what is left is genuinely client code. Refactor the remainder, not the current shape.
